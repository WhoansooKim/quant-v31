# Lit review part 3: ML/LLM return prediction, plus replication, decay and cost evidence (2021–2026)

Compiled 2026-11-14. Scope: (a) machine-learning and LLM short-horizon stock return prediction; (b) replication, decay, trading-cost and factor-timing evidence for the anomaly zoo. Individual anomalies are covered in parts 1, 2 and 4 and are not catalogued here.

**How each figure was checked.** Every figure carries one of these marks:
- **[V]** I checked it against the abstract, the paper text (arXiv or NBER PDF), or the published-journal abstract.
- **[S]** It comes from a secondary summary (CXO, Alpha Architect, Quantpedia, Robeco, a discussant's slides) and I could not check it at the source.
- **[U]** I recall it but could not check it in this session. Do not use these numbers for decisions.

A few pre-2021 papers (GKX 2020, HXZ 2020, Novy-Marx–Velikov 2016, DeMiguel et al. 2020, Patton–Weller 2020, Jacobs–Müller 2020, Haddad–Kozak–Santosh 2020) are included as anchors, because the 2021–2026 literature is mostly a reaction to them.

**Abbreviations.** EW = equal-weighted. VW = value-weighted. L/S = long-short. SR = annualized Sharpe ratio. TC = transaction costs. OOS = out-of-sample.

## Notes and synthesis

### 1. What realistic net edge do ML/LLM methods show for liquid large caps at weekly to monthly horizons?

- **Headline ML Sharpe ratios come from small stocks and are quoted before costs.** GKX's VW L/S SR of 1.35 [V] is the optimistic anchor (1987–2016). Every later stress test shrinks it:
  - Avramov et al.: profits fall substantially once microcaps and distressed stocks are excluded.
  - Cakici et al. (A4): insignificant, slightly *negative* big-firm alphas over 2001–2020.
  - Li–Rossi–Yan–Zheng: real-time signal universes give "only marginal" large-cap performance.
  - Azevedo–Hoegner–Velikov (A6), the most complete accounting: average decay of about 57%, ending at **0.2–1.4%/mo L/S and SRs that "fail to surpass the market" for most models**, with a best net SR of about 0.84 from an LSTM.
  - Jiang–Kelly–Xiu chart CNN: 2.5 EW but **0.6 VW, gross**.
- **Reasonable prior for a well-built monthly ML long-short on large caps, net, post-2005:** SR roughly 0.3–0.8, i.e. 2–6%/yr at 10% volatility. It also needs shorting, a cost-aware optimizer (A8, C7), and survives only as a diversifier. **Long-only** in large caps, the active part is a fraction of that: about 1–3%/yr of tracking alpha, if it survives at all. This is my inference from A2, A4, A6 and A13, not a single paper's number.
- **LLM news signals are real but are a daily, small-cap, low-cost-trader phenomenon.** Lopez-Lira–Tang give the best-documented post-cutoff evidence:
  - Gross SR 2.97, but turnover is about 190%/day.
  - The strategy is unprofitable at 20 bp round-trip.
  - It decayed from SR 6.5 (2021Q4) to 1.2 (H1 2024) as LLM adoption spread [all V].
  - Chen–Kelly–Xiu: large-firm L/S returns are "significantly lower", and the discussant puts net SR at about 1.5 even at an optimistic 10 bp/day.
- **Weekly to monthly large-cap LLM evidence is thin.** The only clean example is Anic et al.: momentum on the S&P 500 with an LLM news filter, OOS SR 0.79 → 1.06 [S], over a short window using the OpenAI API.
- **Foundation time-series models (Chronos, TimesFM) have negative OOS R² on returns** (A14). Kronos-type gains are RankIC over weak baselines, not net P&L. **Deep RL and LLM trading agents have no credible net evidence**, and long-horizon tests (FINSABER, StockBench) show them at or below buy-and-hold.

### 2. Look-ahead and leakage: the single biggest validity issue for LLM papers

- GPT-4o memorizes index levels and macro prints before its cutoff (B9). Masking and "pretend it's date X" prompts do not fix this. Memorization measurably inflates headline-return accuracy pre-cutoff (B11). Distortion is largest for **large, well-known firms** (B8), which is the user's universe.
- Consequence: any backtest of Llama-3, Qwen or Mistral run through Ollama on news from before that model's training cutoff (2023–2024 for current open models) is contaminated.
- Leak-free options:
  - (i) forward paper-trading only;
  - (ii) chronologically consistent open models such as ChronoBERT/ChronoGPT (B10) or DatedGPT;
  - (iii) entity-anonymized prompts, which help only partially.
- Several catalogued papers (B3 OPT, B5 earlier versions, B2 open LLMs) have partial train/test overlap.

### 3. Could a retail system with free data and local open-source LLMs (Ollama, no paid API) capture any of this?

- **Daily news-drift (B1–B4): realistically no.**
  - It needs a real-time paid news feed: Dow Jones/RavenPack/Refinitiv-grade timestamps. Free RSS feeds are late and patchy.
  - It needs auction execution at under 10 bp round-trip, and daily turnover of about 190%.
  - The edge sits in small stocks and has already roughly halved every year since 2021 as adoption spread.
  - Open models can score the sentiment (OPT/LLaMA ≥ FinBERT in B3 and B4), but the **data and execution** constraints bind, not the model.
- **Monthly ML cross-section: possible in principle, small payoff.**
  - The inputs are free: Chen–Zimmermann Open Source AP signals and JKP factors are free downloads; price/volume charts are free.
  - Point-in-time fundamentals and survivorship-free universes are the usual retail gap. Free sources typically have current-constituent survivorship bias, which matches this project's own §22.AO-68 finding.
  - The expected net edge in large caps is a fraction of a Sharpe point, with no shorting and with low-turnover construction required. The honest prior is about 0–2%/yr over the benchmark, with SE of several %/yr; **it cannot be confirmed in under about 5–10 years of live data.**
- **LLM as a filter on an existing low-turnover rule** (Anic et al. pattern, monthly, large caps) is the only LLM use with large-cap, monthly, post-cutoff support.
  - Run locally through Ollama, it must be validated **forward only** because of leakage.
  - The supporting evidence is one paper with a short OOS window and a closed model. Treat it as an experiment, not an edge.

### 4. What the replication literature says about expected net returns

- **Replication itself is not the problem.** JKP and Chen–Zimmermann: about 85–98% of published predictors reproduce in-sample. HXZ's 65% failure rate is mostly a VW/microcap construction effect.
- **Decay and costs are the problem:**
  - OOS performance is about 50% of in-sample (Falck et al.; Chen–Lopez-Lira–Zimmermann 51–53%; McLean–Pontiff −58% post-publication).
  - The US is the only market with a reliable post-publication decline (Jacobs–Müller).
  - **Net of effective spreads, post-2005 and post-publication, the average anomaly's expected return is about 4 bp/mo; the best are about 10 bp; combinations about 20 bp/mo, i.e. about 2.4%/yr, before price impact (Chen–Velikov).** Mutual funds capture no momentum and little value net (Patton–Weller).
- **Factor timing:** academic gross gains of about 2%/yr median per factor (Neuhierl et al.) versus AQR's century study ("modest... likely fails to overcome frictions"). Net, it is roughly zero for a retail implementer.
- **Practical rule implied by the literature:** haircut any backtest by at least 50%, subtract realistic costs at the strategy's actual turnover, and assume the large-cap, long-only residual is near zero unless the signal is low-turnover and economically motivated. This is consistent with the project's own §22.AO-66 finding that an honest runner cannot beat SPY.
