# Lit Review Part 1 — Price/Volume Short-Horizon (1 day–2 months) US Equity Predictability, 2021–2026

Compiled 2026-11-14. Scope: price/volume-only signals (reversal, short/intermediate momentum, technical/trend, CNN-on-charts, volume, vol/IVOL, lottery/MAX, liquidity provision, overnight/intraday, seasonality). Out of scope (other parts): event/information signals, ML/LLM & replication meta-studies, index/ETF timing.

**Method note / limits.** About 45 web searches plus direct fetches of the full text of 3 papers (Medhat–Schmeling RFS accepted version, Dai–Medhat–Novy-Marx–Rizova NBER draft, Jiang–Kelly–Xiu JF). Search ran out at the session cap (200 calls shared across agents), so some cells come from abstracts or secondary summaries (CXO Advisory, Alpha Architect, Robeco). Every number is tagged: **[FT]** = verified in full text, **[Abs]** = abstract/publisher page, **[2nd]** = secondary summary (may come from an earlier draft). "n/r" = not reported in the sources I could reach. Returns are gross unless marked net. L/S = long–short decile/quintile spread. EW/VW = equal-/value-weighted.

**Data column key.** *Free* = can be rebuilt from free daily OHLCV (yfinance) plus shares outstanding / filing dates from SEC EDGAR. *Free\** = free, but needs daily open prices (yfinance opens are usable but noisy, and adjusted-open handling must be checked; see memory note on adjusted vs nominal prices). *Paid* = needs CRSP/TAQ/IBES/proprietary data.

## B. Synthesis

### B1. What survives the target constraints (long-only, S&P 500, 0.07–0.25%/side, next-day-open fills)

1. **Turnover-conditioned short-term momentum (#1).** This is the only short-horizon effect documented to be *strongest* in megacaps: largest-500 +0.42%/mo L/S, megacap ~0.60%/mo net with an implementation lag. Long-only version: buy last-month winners within the top-turnover bucket of the S&P 500. Turnover is ~90%/mo, so at 0.25%/side the round-trip drag is ≈0.45%/mo, which consumes most of a long-leg premium. At 0.07%/side (≈0.13%/mo) it is viable. Implementable with free data.
2. **Moving-average distance, SMA21/SMA200 (#15).** About 9%/yr VW alpha, a **long-side–dominant** effect, $5 price filter, low turnover (slow signal), break-even ≈1%. It sits closest to trend/momentum, so check overlap with the existing core and with 52WH.
3. **Industry-relative, announcement-adjusted reversal conditioned on low turnover (#2, #4, plus de Groot 2012 and Blitz 2013).** Its magnitude is flat across the top four size quintiles, and low-turnover reversals keep building for ~3 months. That allows a 2–6 week hold that dilutes the per-trade cost and does not depend on the first-day bounce lost by next-open fills. Post-2001 IRRX is 58 bps/mo L/S vs 18 bps for plain STR. Long-only: buy industry-relative 5-day–1-month losers that are low-turnover, low-vol and outside earnings windows. Residual-asymmetry evidence (#5) supports the long (loser) side.
4. **Price-image / price-path ML (#6, #7).** Sharpe >1 restricted to the largest 500. Net monthly SR 1.5 EW at 10–20 bps costs. #7 says the edge is strong in the top 500. Free data. The risks are overfitting and that most of the edge sits in week 1; days 6–20 still give SR 0.4–1.2 for a monthly hold.
5. **Composite short-term signals in large caps (#3).** Robeco's large-cap MSCI World evidence: net alpha after cost mitigation, works long-only, robust to several-day lags. Costs still take >2/3 of gross alpha. The value is in *combining* #1–#3 so that turnover is shared (one rebalance carries several signals).

### B2. Main reasons the rest fail for this system
- **Microcap/small-cap dependence**: STREV\* (largest-500 is −2 bps/mo), salience (#13 says it does not exist outside microcaps), MAX/weekly reversal (#21), Robinhood herding (#20: none above $1B), overnight-reversal frequency (#8, EW), high-volume premium.
- **Short-leg (overpricing) effects** that a long-only book cannot harvest: MAX/lottery, IVOL, salience, retail herding, Birru's speculative-leg weekday pattern.
- **First-day / same-close fills**: much of reversal and CNN profit sits in day 1 or week 1. Dai et al. show first-day effects up to ~80 bps on illiquid names. Next-open fills lose these, and many anomalies earn *negative* overnight returns (#10, Lou et al.). Buying at the open also pays the overnight leg that STR does not earn intraday.
- **Costs and decay**: average anomaly net ≈4 bps/mo (#16). Plain STR costs 0.56%/mo and that subsumes its gross (#1). Plain STR post-decimalization is 18 bps (t=0.18) (#2). End-of-day reversal disappeared in 2020–22 and is weakest in the largest stocks (#24). Liquid-US residual reversal does not predict direction OOS (#33).
- **Paid data**: TAQ (#10, #19, #23, #24), IBES revisions (part of #3), proprietary app data (#20).
- **Horizon mismatch**: frog-in-the-pan, 52WH and single-stock TSMOM work at 6–12 months (#17, #27), not as 1-day–2-month swings.
- **Measurement artifacts**: IVOL reversal largely disappears after bid-ask/asynchronicity corrections (#25).

### B3. Implementation cautions specific to this repo (cross-ref memory)
- Turnover needs shares outstanding. yfinance values are current snapshots, which is look-ahead in a backtest; use EDGAR dei quarterly values, point in time.
- Universe: use **point-in-time S&P 500 membership**. Current constituents = survivorship/look-ahead (see memory: redesign-18pct-is-beta).
- Fills: model next-open plus a gap; never same-close (memory: stop-fill-at-gap-open, honest-runner-loses-to-spy).
- Judge by per-trade edge vs SPY at matched exposure, and use time-based validation splits (memory notes).
