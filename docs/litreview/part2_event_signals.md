# Literature Review Part 2 of 4: Event- and Information-Driven Short-Horizon Signals (US equities, 2021–2026)

Scope: signals that fire on an identifiable event or information release, held 1 day to 2 months. Covered: earnings (PEAD, earnings announcement premium, pre-announcement, call text), analysts, insiders (Form 4), 13F and flows, short interest and lending, options, SEC filing text, news and social sentiment, retail order flow, index changes, buybacks and dividends, M&A, FDA, and FOMC/macro days at the stock level.
Not covered here (other parts): pure price/volume signals, ML/LLM and replication meta-studies, index/ETF timing.

Compiled 2026-11-14. **Method note, read first:** about 45 web searches plus direct fetches of abstracts and PDFs. The session's shared web-search budget ran out partway through, so some later topics (buybacks, dividends, M&A, FDA, 13F) got fewer verified 2021+ papers than planned. Each row has a **Verif.** column:
- **V** = numbers checked against the paper's own text (PDF or abstract).
- **A** = checked against the abstract or a journal/RePEc/NBER landing page only.
- **S** = from a secondary summary (Alpha Architect, Quantpedia, CXO, Substack). Treat as provisional.
- **M** = metadata from memory, not re-checked this session. Check before citing.

Target system: long-only, S&P 500 universe, swing holding (days to weeks), entry at the next-day open, costs of 0.07–0.25% per side, free data only (EDGAR, yfinance, FINRA short interest, free earnings calendars).

## 2. Coverage gaps (be explicit)

- **Analyst revisions and recommendations:** no verified 2021+ US paper with clean return magnitudes found before the search budget ran out. Known: Bradshaw et al. (2022 WP, "Predictability of Analyst Stock Recommendation Revisions") find that *predictable* upgrades get smaller reactions. Kecskés-Michaely-Womack (2017, MS) and Loh-Stulz (2011/2018, "Is sell-side research more valuable in bad times?" JF) are anchors. IBES (paid) is required. Free proxies (Finviz/Yahoo rating changes) have no point-in-time history. Recommendation-change reactions happen mostly at the open on day 0, so a next-open entry gets the drift only.
- **13F cloning and flow-induced trading:** only pre-2021 anchors found (Lou 2012 RFS flow-induced trading; Verbeek & Wang 2013; Agarwal et al. 2013 confidential holdings; Wardlaw 2020 JF shows the flow-pressure measure is mechanically contaminated). The 45-day filing lag makes 13F a quarterly signal, not a swing signal.
- **SEC filing text ("Lazy Prices"):** Cohen, Malloy & Nguyen (2020, JF 75(3)) report up to 188 bp/month alpha for non-changers minus changers, with returns accruing slowly over months. No verified 2021+ post-publication replication was found. The signal is free (EDGAR 10-K/10-Q diffs) but has a **monthly to quarterly horizon** and mostly works through the short leg (changers underperform).
- **8-K item signals:** older work (item-level drift up to 90 days on 2004–06 data; EDGAR-log demand for unscheduled 8-Ks predicts lower returns, 2003–16). Nothing recent verified.
- **Conference-call tone:** Druz, Wagner & Zeckhauser (NBER w20991, 2015) report post-call drift on managerial tone. Modern versions are LLM/ML-based (Part 3).

## 3. Assessment for the target system

Constraints: long-only S&P 500, next-day-open entry, 0.07–0.25% per side, free data.

### The 5 most promising (ranked, all still unproven for this system)

1. **Pre-earnings-announcement premium with an uncertainty sort** (#9 Gao-Hu-Zhang 2025 MS; EAP anchors Frazzini-Lamont, Barber et al.). Buy S&P 500 names roughly 10 trading days before a *confirmed* EA, ranking on a free uncertainty proxy (trailing realized vol, or the average absolute reaction to the last 4–8 EAs). Exit at the close before the EA. Why it fits: scheduled event (no disclosure lag, no overnight-gap capture problem), long-side return, EAP reported in large caps, holding period inside the swing window, and no earnings-gap risk. Gross 0.3–1.5% per 10 days against about 0.14–0.5% round trip. Risks: needs point-in-time confirmed EA dates, and the free-proxy decile may be much weaker than the IV sort.
2. **Earnings-announcement-return drift, long leg only** (#3 PEAD.txt: the simple EA-day abnormal-return quintile spread gave 1.65% over 63 days in 2010–19; most of PEAD.txt's excess came from the long quintile). Rule: next-open entry after a top-quintile EA-day abnormal return, held about 20–60 days. Free (prices plus EA dates). Counter-evidence: Martineau (#1) and Subrahmanyam (#2) find SUE-based PEAD dead outside microcaps since about 2006, so a large-cap EAR drift may be near zero. Test before believing.
3. **EA-date timing as a filter** (#12 Johnson & So 2018; Noh-So-Verdi 2021). Avoid or exclude names whose confirmed EA date slips versus their historical slot; prefer names that report early. Cheap, free in principle, and usable as an overlay on candidates 1 and 2. The data problem is point-in-time confirmed-date history.
4. **Insider cluster or officer open-market purchases (Form 4 code P)** (#13–16). EDGAR is free and well structured. Expect most of the alpha to be gone at a next-open entry (Ozlen-Batumoglu: 70–80% happens before filing; Zhao: monthly alpha falls from 2.10% to an insignificant 0.68% once day 1 is skipped), and large-cap insider trades carry little information (Cziraki-Gider, Lakonishok-Lee). Worth testing only as a secondary tilt or tie-breaker.
5. **Short-interest / days-to-cover exclusion filter (FINRA, free)** (#18, #20). Anomaly profits sit in the short leg and in high-fee stocks (Muravyev-Pearson-Pollet), so a long-only S&P 500 book cannot harvest them. It can avoid the worst names (top-decile DTC or a recent SI spike). The filter must use the publication date (about 8 business days after settlement), not the settlement date.

### Main failure reasons for the rest

- **Disclosure/overnight gap capture:** index additions (#28), insider filings (#13–16), recommendation changes, and M&A or FDA jumps all reprice at the first open after release. A next-open entry gets only the leftover, which is near zero in large caps.
- **Decay after publication or after a regime change:** PEAD-SUE (#1–2), the index effect (#28), pre-FOMC drift (#34), WSB DD and social sentiment after GME (#26–27), and the US buyback drift (#29).
- **Short-leg-only or small-cap-only returns:** short-interest and borrow-fee anomalies (#18, #20), Robinhood herding (#22), retail OIB (#25), 10b5-1 red flags (#17), and microcap insider buys (#14).
- **Per-event edge below costs:** social sentiment at 2–8 bp/SD over 1 day (#27), overnight EA premium of 8 bp (#11), FOMC-day L/S of 31 bp gross (#33) against 0.14–0.5% round trip.
- **Paid or discontinued data:** OptionMetrics (#9 IV version, #10, #21, #33), IBES SUE/revisions, TAQ retail flow (#24–25), Capital IQ transcripts (#3), Robintrack (#22–23), and StockTwits/X APIs (#27).
- **Horizon mismatch:** buybacks (years), Lazy Prices (months to quarters), 13F (quarterly with a 45-day lag), and merger arb (deal close often exceeds 2 months).

### Test hygiene (from this project's own lessons)

Fill at the next open, including gap-through stops. Model commission plus spread at the high end (0.25%). Use point-in-time S&P 500 membership. Use point-in-time EA dates, since look-ahead in EA timestamps (after-close vs pre-open) is the most common bug in this literature. Judge on per-trade edge against the median, not annual CAGR. Compare against SPY over the same window.
